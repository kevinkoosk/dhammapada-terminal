# Chapter 8: Better Than a Hundred Years

![](images/image-2.jpg)

(100)
A single word, if it brings peace,
is better than a thousand that don’t mean anything.

(101)
One verse that awakens truth
is better than a thousand verses of fluff.

(102)
One line of real Dhamma—
truly heard, truly felt—
beats a hundred verses that don’t touch the heart.

(103)
You could win a thousand battles—
but if you haven’t conquered yourself,
you’ve won nothing.

(104–105)
Victory over yourself?
That’s the win no god, demon, or spirit can undo.
That’s forever.

(106)
A hundred years of rituals and offerings
can’t compare to one sincere moment
honoring someone truly free.

(107)
Even tending sacred fires for a century—
doesn’t equal one moment of deep reverence
toward someone awakened.

(108)
You could give gifts every day, all year—
but honoring someone who walks the true path
brings more good than all of it.

(109)
When you honor your elders—
with a sincere heart—
four blessings follow:
long life, beauty, joy, and strength.

(110)
One day of pure, mindful living
is better than a hundred years
of just existing without control.

(111)
One day of wisdom and clarity
beats a hundred years of thoughtless wandering.

(112)
One day of deep effort and courage
is worth more than a century of laziness.

(113)
One day of truly seeing
how everything rises and falls—
that’s more valuable
than a hundred years of sleepwalking.

(114)
To glimpse the Deathless,
even for a moment—
that’s worth more than a whole life
lived in the dark.

(115)
One clear sight
of the highest truth
is more precious
than a hundred years
without ever seeing it.



This copy of the Dhammapada is paraphrased and modernized from the translation by Acharya Buddharakkhita. (Originally published by Buddha Dharma Education Association Inc).
