# Chapter 11: This Body Will Fade

![](images/image-5.jpg)

(146)
The world is burning—
so why are you laughing?
Why are you celebrating in the dark?
Can’t you see the fire?
Don’t you want the light?

(147)
Look at this body—
painted skin covering wounds and cravings.
It breaks, it aches,
and nothing about it lasts.

(148)
This body gets used up—
a fragile little nest of illness.
And in the end, it falls apart.
Death always wins.

(149)
These bones, pale and dry,
scattered like empty fruit shells in the fall—
how can you look at them
and still feel desire?

(150)
This body is a city—
built from bone, covered in flesh.
Inside?
Decay. Death. Pride. Envy.
All crowded in.

(151)
Even the fanciest royal chariots break down.
And so does your body.
But the Dhamma—the truth—
never ages.
And the wise keep sharing it,
generation after generation.

(152)
A man with no wisdom
just grows old like a bull—
bigger, yes,
but not better.

(153)
Through lifetime after lifetime
I searched—
looking for the one who built this house of suffering.
Again and again I was born.
Again and again I suffered.

(154)
But now I see you, house-builder.
I won’t let you build again.
The rafters are broken, the ridgepole is gone.
My mind is free.
I’ve destroyed craving.
I’m done with rebirth.

(155)
If you waste your youth—
neither walking the path
nor building your life—
you’ll end up like an old crane
standing alone in a dry pond
with no fish.

(156)
If you waste your youth chasing nothing,
you’ll look back,
regretful and exhausted—
like a broken arrow
shot from a worn-out bow.



This copy of the Dhammapada is paraphrased and modernized from the translation by Acharya Buddharakkhita. (Originally published by Buddha Dharma Education Association Inc).
