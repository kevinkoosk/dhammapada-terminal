# Chapter 14: The Buddha

![](images/image-2.jpg)

(179)
How do you follow someone
who leaves no trace?
The Buddha moves without limits—
no one can undo his victory,
no craving can chase him.

(180)
There’s no trail to track him.
No desire clings to him.
He’s free from the cycles of becoming.
He’s beyond reach.

(181)
Those who love meditation,
who embrace stillness and simplicity—
these awakened ones,
even the gods bow to them.

(182)
It’s rare to be born as a human.
It’s rare to hear the truth.
It’s rare to meet a Buddha.
Don’t waste this chance.

(183)
Here’s the Buddha’s teaching, in full:
Avoid evil.
Do good.
Clean your mind.
That’s it.

(184)
Patience is the highest practice.
And Nibbāna is the highest truth.
If you harm others,
you’re no monk—
no matter what robes you wear.

(185)
Don’t insult others.
Don’t hurt them.
Live simply.
Eat in moderation.
Seek solitude.
Meditate.
This is the path the Buddhas walked.

(186–187)
Chasing pleasure is a trap.
Even if gold rains down from the sky—
you’ll never feel full.
Pleasure brings a flash of joy,
but leaves a shadow of pain.
The wise know better.
They choose freedom.

(188)
Some run to the hills,
the groves,
the temples—
looking for safety.

(189)
But those places aren’t true refuge.
They don’t free you from suffering.

(190–191)
Real refuge is this:
The Buddha,
his teachings,
and the community.
They show you the Four Noble Truths—
suffering, its end,
and the path to peace.

(192)
That’s the refuge that works.
That’s the refuge that frees you.

(193)
It’s rare for a true sage to be born.
But when he is,
his presence lifts the whole world.

(194)
Blessed is the birth of a Buddha.
Blessed is the sharing of truth.
Blessed is harmony in the community.
Blessed is the shared search for freedom.

(195–196)
If you honor the peaceful ones—
the Buddhas and their disciples,
those who’ve left sorrow behind—
your merit can’t be measured.
It shines beyond calculation.



This copy of the Dhammapada is paraphrased and modernized from the translation by Acharya Buddharakkhita. (Originally published by Buddha Dharma Education Association Inc).
