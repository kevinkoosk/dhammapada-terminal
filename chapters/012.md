# Chapter 12: Master Yourself

![](images/image-6.jpg)

(157)
If you truly care about yourself—
pay attention.
Watch yourself like a guardian,
in every moment,
even in the quiet hours of the night.

(158)
Get yourself right first.
Then help others.
That way, no one can call you a hypocrite.

(159)
Practice what you preach.
If you want to guide others,
start with mastering yourself.
And that’s no easy thing.

(160)
You are your own protector.
No one else can do it for you.
Only when you master yourself
will you find the freedom you’re looking for.

(161)
A fool’s worst enemy
is his own mind—
his own harmful choices
crushing him
like a diamond grinding stone.

(162)
Like a vine choking the tree it climbs,
so does a wicked person
destroy themselves from the inside.

(163)
Bad stuff is easy.
Hurting yourself is easy.
Doing good?
That’s what’s hard—
but that’s what makes you whole.

(164)
Mocking the teachings of the wise
only brings ruin.
Like a bamboo that bears fruit just once,
then dies—
so does the fool destroy himself.

(165)
You create your own downfall.
You create your own cleansing.
No one else can do it for you.
Purity or impurity—
that’s on you.

(166)
Don’t sacrifice your own well-being
for someone else’s path—
not even for someone great.
Know what’s good for you.
Then go all in on it.



This copy of the Dhammapada is paraphrased and modernized from the translation by Acharya Buddharakkhita. (Originally published by Buddha Dharma Education Association Inc).
