# Chapter 10: No One Wants to Die

![](images/image-4.jpg)

(129)
Everyone fears pain.
Everyone fears death.
So put yourself in their shoes—
don’t kill,
and don’t push others to do it either.

(130)
Every living being wants to stay alive.
So treat them how you’d want to be treated.
No one wants to be hurt.

(131)
If you hurt others while chasing your own happiness,
you’ll never find real happiness.

(132)
But if you care for others,
even while you pursue joy,
peace will find its way to you.

(133)
Don’t use harsh words.
They bounce back.
Anger breeds anger—
and pain circles around.

(134)
But if you stay silent,
like a broken bell,
and let go of revenge—
you’ve come close to freedom.

(135)
Just like a herder drives cattle with a stick,
old age and death
drive us all forward
from life to life.

(136)
Fools don’t understand
that their bad deeds burn.
They feel the pain later—
like touching a flame too late.

(137)
If you hurt those who are peaceful and defenseless,
you’re bringing trouble to your own door.

(138–140)
Expect one of ten things:
a painful illness,
a violent accident,
losing your mind,
a government crackdown,
a lawsuit,
the death of loved ones,
your home burned down,
your fortune wiped out—
or after death,
a descent into hell.

(141)
It’s not about dreadlocks,
dirt, fasting, or rituals.
You can’t scrub away your past
if your mind is still full of doubt.

(142)
You can wear clean clothes,
speak gently, and live in peace.
If you’ve let go of violence,
you’re a true monk—
not because of what you wear,
but because of who you are.

(143)
It’s rare to meet someone
who’s humble and disciplined.
Just like it’s rare to find
a horse that doesn’t need the whip.

(144)
So be like that horse—awake and ready.
Fuel your spirit with faith,
discipline, deep focus, and clear wisdom.
Let your mind rise above this suffering.

(145)
Irrigators move rivers.
Bow-makers shape arrows.
Woodworkers carve.
And the wise?
They shape themselves.
