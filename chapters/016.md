# Chapter 16: The Bonds of Affection

![](images/image-4.jpg)

(209)
When you chase what should be avoided,
and skip what really matters,
you’ll end up bitter—
jealous of those who stayed true to themselves.

(210)
Don’t get too close to what you love.
And don’t obsess over what you hate.
Both will hurt—
either by leaving you,
or staying too long.

(211)
Cling to nothing.
What you love will leave.
What you hate will appear.
Only when you stop clinging
do you stop suffering.

(212)
Grief is born from attachment.
So is fear.
No attachment =
no grief,
no fear.

(213)
Affection brings grief.
Affection brings fear.
Let go of it all,
and what’s left?
Freedom.

(214)
Same goes for emotional attachment.
Same goes for lust.
Same goes for craving.
If you cut the cord—
there’s no grief, no fear.

(215)
From lust comes suffering.
From lust comes anxiety.
But when you free yourself from it—
you rise.

(216)
Craving causes fear.
Letting go causes peace.
Simple as that.

(217)
People admire
those who live with wisdom,
with virtue,
and who quietly do
what’s right.

(218)
If your heart’s pointed toward Nibbāna,
if your mind has risen above desires—
you’re swimming upstream
toward freedom.

(219)
When someone returns home after a long journey,
family and friends welcome him back
with open arms.

(220)
And when a good person
leaves this world—
their good deeds
welcome them home
in the next.



This copy of the Dhammapada is paraphrased and modernized from the translation by Acharya Buddharakkhita. (Originally published by Buddha Dharma Education Association Inc).
