# Chapter 15: The Art of Happiness

![](images/image-3.jpg)

(197)
We can live joyfully,
even among those filled with hate—
if we stay friendly,
and choose love anyway.

(198)
We can be at peace,
even around people consumed by cravings—
if we don’t let that craving touch us.

(199)
We can live freely,
even among the greedy—
if we’re content with less.

(200)
We can be happy with nothing.
We can feed on joy,
like radiant beings
who need no possessions.

(201)
Winning makes enemies.
Losing brings pain.
But those who let go of both
find peace—
because they don’t need to win.

(202)
Nothing burns like desire.
Nothing cuts like hate.
No burden is heavier than existence.
And nothing compares to
the calm of Nibbāna.

(203)
Hunger is the worst sickness.
Being trapped in this cycle—
the worst pain.
See this clearly,
and you’ll know:
freedom is the highest bliss.

(204)
Health is the real wealth.
Contentment is the real treasure.
A good friend is your real family.
And the highest happiness—
is freedom from craving.

(205)
Taste the stillness of solitude.
Let truth cleanse you.
There, in the quiet,
you’ll drink the deep joy
of real peace.

(206)
Being near the wise
feels like being home.
Even being away from fools
feels like a blessing.

(207)
Fools bring grief.
Being around them
is like partnering with an enemy.
But the wise—
they lift you up,
like long-lost family.

(208)
So walk with the noble ones—
steady, kind, and clear.
Follow them the way
the moon follows the stars.



This copy of the Dhammapada is paraphrased and modernized from the translation by Acharya Buddharakkhita. (Originally published by Buddha Dharma Education Association Inc).
