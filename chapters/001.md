# Chapter 1: It Starts In The Mind

![](images/image-1.jpg)

(1)
Everything starts in your mind.
Your thoughts shape your words, your actions, your world.
If your mindset is toxic, your life will feel heavy—
pain will follow you like a cart stuck to an ox.

(2)
Everything starts in your mind.
Your vibe leads the way.
If your heart is clear and kind,
joy follows you like your shadow on a sunny day—never leaving your side.

(3)
“He dissed me. She hit me. They humiliated me. I was ripped off.”
If you keep replaying that hurt,
you’re just feeding the hate—it won’t go away.

(4)
“He dissed me. She hit me. They humiliated me. I was ripped off.”
If you don’t keep replaying that hurt,
the hate loses its grip. You’re free.

(5)
You can’t heal hate with more hate.
Only kindness can cool that fire.
That’s not just advice—it’s universal truth.

(6)
Some people live like they’ll never die.
But those who know life is short
don’t waste time holding grudges.

(7)
If you're chasing pleasure like it's everything—
no discipline, no balance, just binge and scroll—
life will hit you like a storm taking down a brittle tree.
That’s when Mara wins.

(8)
But if you're grounded—
mindful of life’s messiness,
eating just enough, controlling your cravings,
and showing up for what matters—
even Mara can’t shake you.
You’re the mountain. Not the storm.

(9)
Wearing robes doesn’t make you wise.
If you lie, cheat, or have no control over yourself,
you’re just cosplaying enlightenment.

(10)
But if you’ve cleaned out the rot inside,
live with truth, and walk with self-awareness—
you’ve earned every thread of that robe.

(11)
If you’re chasing what doesn’t matter
and ignoring what does,
your mind’s lost in the fog.
You’ll never find what’s real.

(12)
But if you see clearly—
what’s worth your energy and what’s just noise—
you’ll move through life with focus.
You’ll find the real treasure.

(13)
An untrained mind is like a leaky roof.
Lust seeps in, flooding everything.

(14)
But a trained mind?
Like a house built tight and strong.
No storm can get in. Desire can’t drown you.

(15)
The one who does wrong suffers—
now and later.
He looks back at what he did and aches inside.
That regret follows him everywhere.

(16)
The one who does good smiles—
now and later.
She looks back and feels proud,
like sunshine warming her soul.

(17)
When you mess up and know it—
you suffer twice.
Once from the act, and again from the memory.
And when it catches up with you in the next life,
the pain runs deep.

(18)
But if you’ve done right—
you shine now, and you shine later.
The thought “I did good” lifts you up.
And when this life ends, the joy keeps going.

(19)
You can memorize every quote,
chant every verse,
and still miss the point.
Like someone counting other people’s money—
you won’t get any of it.

(20)
But even if you only learn a little,
if you live the truth—
letting go of craving, anger, and illusions—
and hold onto nothing, not even your ego—
you’re already rich in spirit.



This copy of the Dhammapada is paraphrased and modernized from the translation by Acharya Buddharakkhita. (Originally published by Buddha Dharma Education Association Inc).
