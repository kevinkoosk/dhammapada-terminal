# Chapter 26: The Holy Man

![](images/image-2.jpg)

(383)
Exert yourself, O seeker.
Cut the river of craving.
Let go of the senses.
Know what is fleeting,
and walk toward what never dies.

(384)
When a seeker climbs both paths—
stillness and wisdom—
he sees the truth,
and all his chains fall away.

(385)
If you no longer cling
to this shore or the other,
nor even the in-between—
and you live with peace,
untouched and free—
then I call you holy.

(386)
He who is still,
stainless,
and done with the struggle,
free of defilement,
having reached the peak—
him do I call a holy man.

(387)
The sun lights up the day.
The moon lights up the night.
A soldier glows in armor.
But the seeker glows in meditation—
and the Buddha,
he shines always,
day and night.

(388)
The one who has let go of wrong
is a holy man.
The one who walks gently
is a recluse.
The one who has cast off impurities
is a renunciate.

(389)
Don’t strike a seeker.
And a true seeker, when struck,
doesn’t strike back.
Shame to the one who hits,
but even more to the one who hates.

(390)
A seeker finds peace
not by clinging to what is sweet,
but by letting go
of the urge to hurt.
As harm fades,
so too does suffering.

(391)
He who harms no one—
by deed, word, or thought—
is restrained in all ways.
He is truly holy.

(392)
Just as one honors
the fire of sacrifice,
honor those who gave you
the gift of truth.

(393)
Not by hair or robes
or by birth are you made pure.
Only truth,
only the heart that walks upright,
makes a holy man.

(394)
What’s the use of outward shows—
of dreadlocks and skins—
if your mind is tangled in desire?

(395)
He wears robes made from rags.
His ribs show.
He meditates alone in the woods.
He is at peace.
Him I call a holy man.

(396)
Not family name,
not noble birth,
makes a person noble.
One bound by greed is just a proud man.
The free are the holy.

(397)
He who has dropped all chains
and no longer trembles—
he who is released from grip and grasp—
he walks the world light.
He is holy.

(398)
He who has destroyed hatred, craving, and illusion,
torn out their roots,
and awakened to what is real—
him do I call holy.

(399)
He who endures abuse and beating
without anger,
whose true strength is patience—
him do I call a holy man.

(400)
Without anger, without craving,
with quiet strength
and nothing more to prove—
he carries his final body.
He is truly holy.

(401)
He who does not cling—
not to pleasure, not to pain—
is like a droplet on a lotus leaf.
He slips through the world untouched.

(402)
He who finds freedom
here and now,
lays down his burdens,
and walks free—
him do I call a holy man.

(403)
He who is wise,
knows the path and its pitfalls,
and walks it to the end—
he has arrived.
He is holy.

(404)
He who lives with few wants,
wanders freely,
attached to no one—
he is free.

(405)
He who harms no being,
not strong nor small—
who kills no one and causes no death—
him do I call a holy man.

(406)
Among the hateful,
he is loving.
Among the violent,
he is calm.
Among the greedy,
he stands free.

(407)
He who has cast off lust, anger,
pride and lies
like a mustard seed slipping off a needle—
he is clean.
He is holy.

(408)
He speaks with care.
He speaks with truth.
He speaks only to help.
He curses no one.

(409)
He takes nothing not given—
not big nor small,
not good nor bad.
What’s not his,
he leaves untouched.

(410)
He wants nothing
from this world or the next.
He is empty of desire.
He is free.

(411)
He who clings to nothing,
whose heart holds no doubt,
and who has fallen
into the Deathless—
him do I call a holy man.

(412)
He who is beyond gain and loss,
beyond grief,
beyond stain—
he has gone beyond.

(413)
He is clear like the full moon,
calm and still.
He no longer clings
to the taste of life.
He is holy.

(414)
He who crossed the flood
and found the other shore—
who meditates, is calm, and clings to nothing—
him do I call a holy man.

(415)
He who left the life of the house
for the path of no return,
who abandoned craving and becoming—
him do I call a holy man.

(416)
He who has cut the root,
left the world,
and ended the restless wandering—
he is free.

(417)
He who cast off
both human and divine chains
and now walks
with nothing holding him—
he is holy.

(418)
He who is calm,
without liking or disliking,
who has conquered the world—
he walks untouched.

(419)
He who sees all births,
all deaths,
and walks free through them,
without clinging—
he is blessed.

(420)
No one sees his path—
not gods, not angels, not men.
He has burned the seeds of becoming.
He is done.

(421)
He holds nothing.
Not the past.
Not the future.
Not even the present.
He is free.

(422)
He is noble.
He is brave.
He is a sage.
He is beyond passion,
beyond need,
beyond illusion.
He is awake.

(423)
He remembers his past lives,
sees heaven and hell,
and sees the end of birth.
He has reached the summit.
He is a sage.
He is a holy man.



This copy of the Dhammapada is paraphrased and modernized from the translation by Acharya Buddharakkhita. (Originally published by Buddha Dharma Education Association Inc).
