# Chapter 20: The Path

![](images/image-2.jpg)

(273)
Of all paths,
the Eightfold Path is best.
Of all truths,
the Four Noble Truths are the clearest.
Of all states,
freedom from craving is highest.
Of all people—
the Awakened One stands alone.

(274)
There is no other path.
Walk this one,
and Mara won’t know what hit him.

(275)
I’ve walked it.
I’ve pulled out the thorn of lust.
Now I’m pointing the way.
You can end suffering too.

(276)
Only you can walk it.
The Buddhas can show you the trail,
but your feet must move.

(277)
Everything that’s put together
comes apart.
See this,
and the pain begins to fade.

(278)
Everything built from desire
disappoints.
See this,
and you’ll begin to let go.

(279)
Everything is not what it seems—
not self, not yours.
See this,
and you’re on the way to peace.

(280)
If you’re young and strong
but lazy—
daydreaming instead of doing—
you’ll never find the path.

(281)
Watch your words.
Train your mind.
Don’t act out.
Clean up how you speak, think, and live—
and the path will open to you.

(282)
Wisdom grows from meditation.
Without stillness, it withers.
So stay steady,
and your wisdom will bloom.

(283)
Chop down the forest of lust—
not the tree, but the craving.
That's where fear hides.
Clear it out.

(284)
Even a tiny desire
can bind you like a calf to its mother.
Cut it out, or stay stuck.

(285)
Pluck out attachment
like a lotus in autumn—
clean, firm, and final.
Then walk the path to peace.

(286)
“I’ll live here in the rainy season,
there in the winter,
and over there in the summer…”
says the fool,
not seeing death just around the corner.

(287)
Death floods the world
like a river sweeping away
a sleeping village.
And we still cling to our stuff.

(288)
No family can save you
when death knocks.
Not your kids.
Not your father.
Not your tribe.

(289)
So wise up.
Live clean.
And start clearing the path
to Nibbāna.
