# Chapter 22: The State of Woe

![](images/image-4.jpg)

(306)
Liars go down.
So do those who deny their wrongs.
In the end,
they face the same fate.

(307)
There are plenty of people
in holy robes
with unholy hearts.
They’re headed for suffering.

(308)
Better to swallow molten metal
than to take food from kind people
and lie about who you are.

(309)
Cheating with someone else’s partner
brings four curses:
shame, bad dreams,
a ruined name,
and a hellish rebirth.

(310)
The pleasure is short.
The fallout is long.
Kings punish.
Karma punishes.
Don’t go there.

(311)
A monk’s life, badly lived,
is like grasping a blade the wrong way—
it cuts you deep.

(312)
If your vows are fake,
if your restraint is shallow,
you’ll get little from it—
just more confusion.

(313)
If you're going to live this path,
live it with strength.
Half-hearted monks stir up more trouble
than peace.

(314)
Don’t do the bad thing
you’ll regret forever.
Do the good thing
you’ll be grateful for tomorrow.

(315)
Guard yourself
like a city on the border.
Let nothing slip through.
Because when death comes—
you’ll wish you hadn’t waited.

(316)
Ashamed of the wrong things,
and proud of the wrong things—
that kind of twisted thinking
leads straight to suffering.

(317)
Afraid where there’s nothing to fear,
fearless where there is danger—
upside-down views
bring pain.

(318)
Seeing evil where there’s none,
and ignoring evil where it’s real—
is a fast path to sorrow.

(319)
But seeing clearly—
right is right, wrong is wrong—
this brings peace.
This brings joy.
This leads to the good places.
