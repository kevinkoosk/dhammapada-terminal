# Chapter 21: The Wise Way to Live

![](images/image-3.jpg)

(290)
Let go of small pleasures
if it means finding a deeper joy.
The wise know
not all happiness is worth chasing.

(291)
If you hurt others for your own gain,
you’ll never escape hate.
It just wraps tighter.

(292)
When you skip what’s right
and chase what’s wrong—
your inner mess just grows.

(293)
But if you stay alert,
mindful of your body and choices—
you’ll clear out what weighs you down.

(294)
The holy one walks free,
having slain craving (mother),
ego (father),
extreme views (two kings),
and the pull of the senses (the land and treasurer).
He’s at peace.

(295)
He also cuts down the five hindrances—
like killing a wild beast.
No more fear, no more grief.

(296)
Disciples of the Buddha wake with joy—
day and night—
when they remember who he truly was.

(297)
They wake with joy
when they reflect on the truth (Dhamma).

(298)
They wake with joy
when they remember the community (Sangha).

(299)
They wake with joy
when they’re mindful of their bodies.

(300)
They wake with joy
when they live gently and harm no one.

(301)
They wake with joy
when they meditate deeply and often.

(302)
It’s hard to be a monk.
It’s also hard to live at home.
Life with people who don’t understand you hurts.
And wandering without direction hurts more.
So don’t wander aimlessly.
Don’t chase suffering.

(303)
Someone with faith, goodness,
a good name, and honest wealth—
they’re respected anywhere they go.

(304)
The good shine bright,
like mountains in the sun.
The wicked?
They hide in the dark,
like arrows loosed at night.

(305)
The one who walks alone,
rests alone,
sits alone,
tames their mind alone—
finds peace
in the quiet of the forest.



This copy of the Dhammapada is paraphrased and modernized from the translation by Acharya Buddharakkhita. (Originally published by Buddha Dharma Education Association Inc).
