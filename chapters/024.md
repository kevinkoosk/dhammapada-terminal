# Chapter 24: Craving

![](images/image-6.jpg)

(334)
Desire grows like a vine
in a mind that’s careless.
Like a monkey leaping from tree to tree,
we swing from one life to the next.

(335)
When craving takes over,
sorrow multiplies—
like weeds in the rainy season.

(336)
But when you tear out the root of craving,
pain slides off you—
like water off a lotus leaf.

(337)
Listen well:
Dig out craving at the root—
like a hunter searching for fragrant herbs.
Don’t let Mara pull you under,
again and again.

(338)
Cut down a tree,
and it grows back
if the root’s still there.
It’s the same with craving—
it brings you back to suffering
unless you dig deep.

(339)
There are 36 rivers of craving
rushing toward pleasures.
They sweep the foolish man away
like a flood of longing.

(340)
Everywhere you turn, craving grows—
twisting, curling like a vine.
The wise cut it at the root
with clarity and truth.

(341)
Pleasure flows in
from every direction.
It feels sweet—
but it binds you to birth, aging, and death.

(342)
Craving makes people scatter—
like rabbits in a trap.
They’re caught in loops of suffering,
over and over.

(343)
So if you want freedom,
break the trap.
Uproot your craving.
Escape the snare.

(344)
Some renounce the world—
only to come running back.
Look at that man:
free,
yet running back to chains.

(345–346)
Iron chains aren’t the worst kind.
What really binds you
is longing—
for jewelry, family, lovers.
These cords seem loose,
but they pull tight.
The wise break them and walk free.

(347)
Desire spins a web—
and the craving fool
crawls right back into it.
But the wise cut it down
and leave the pain behind.

(348)
Let go of past,
future,
and even now.
Cross to the far shore—
and never be born again.

(349)
The man full of passion
feeds his craving daily.
His thoughts chain him.
His mind traps him.

(350)
But the one who faces down his thoughts,
who reflects deeply,
and watches his mind—
he tears down Mara’s walls
and ends the hunger.

(351)
The one who’s free—
fearless, without craving,
with the thorns of existence pulled out—
this is his final life.

(352)
Who understands the truth deeply,
knows the teachings in order,
and sees clearly—
he’s done.
He’s wise.
This is his final body.

(353)
I’ve seen everything.
I’ve beaten everything.
And I’m not chained by any of it.
Freed by letting go,
I call no one my master—
because I’ve walked the whole path
alone.

(354)
The gift of truth beats any other gift.
The taste of truth is sweeter than honey.
The joy of truth outshines all joys.
And the one freed from craving
feels no more pain.

(355)
Wealth destroys fools—
but not the wise.
Fools ruin themselves
and everyone around them
for the sake of riches.

(356)
Weeds ruin crops.
Lust ruins people.
Gifts to the lustless
bear the best fruit.

(357)
Weeds ruin crops.
Hatred ruins people.
Gifts to the hate-free
bear the best fruit.

(358)
Weeds ruin crops.
Delusion ruins people.
Gifts to the clear-minded
bear the best fruit.

(359)
Weeds ruin crops.
Greed ruins people.
Gifts to those who want nothing
bear the best fruit.
