# Chapter 7: The Free One

![](images/image-1.jpg)

(90)
For the one who’s finished the journey—
no more fire of desire,
no more sorrow,
no more chains.
Only peace.

(91)
The mindful live light.
They don’t cling to any home.
Like swans leaving lake after lake—
they keep flying,
free.

(92)
Those who let go of hoarding,
who eat with awareness,
who seek the formless,
the free—
their path leaves no footprint,
like birds in the sky.

(93)
Even Mara can’t trace them—
the one who’s dropped craving for food,
for ego,
for this world.
Their path is invisible.

(94)
Even the gods admire
the one who’s mastered their senses—
like a charioteer with calm horses.
Pride gone,
wounds healed,
free.

(95)
For the awakened one,
life in this world has ended.
No more anger,
no more mud in the heart.
He’s steady like a pillar,
clear like a still pool.

(96)
His thoughts are still.
His words are calm.
His actions are clean.
He is fully awake—
tranquil, wise,
and free.

(97)
No blind faith.
No craving.
No karma-making.
No strings attached.
This one knows what isn’t made.
He’s the real deal.

(98)
Anywhere they walk—
forest, hill, or village—
the presence of such a person
makes the place feel sacred.

(99)
Where others see nothing special,
the free soul finds joy.
They don’t need sensual pleasures.
They’re home in the quiet.



This copy of the Dhammapada is paraphrased and modernized from the translation by Acharya Buddharakkhita. (Originally published by Buddha Dharma Education Association Inc).
