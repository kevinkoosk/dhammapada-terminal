# Chapter 6: The Way of the Wise

![](images/image-6.jpg)

(76)
If someone shows you your flaws
and calls you out—
follow them.
They’re like a guide
leading you to hidden treasure.

(77)
The one who corrects you, protects you.
They may not be “popular,”
but the wise love them.
Only the immature push them away.

(78)
Don’t hang with the toxic.
Don’t follow the cruel.
Walk with the kind,
the good-hearted,
the clear-eyed.

(79)
The wise drink from the Dhamma
like water on a hot day.
They feel calm,
deeply calm—
just being near the truth.

(80)
Irrigators move rivers.
Bow-makers bend arrows.
Carpenters carve wood.
The wise?
They shape themselves.

(81)
A rock doesn’t flinch in a storm.
That’s how the wise are—
unshaken by praise or blame.

(82)
When the wise hear the truth,
they become still and clear—
like a deep, quiet lake.

(83)
The wise don’t chase stuff.
They don’t get dramatic over pain or joy.
They walk steady,
with no hunger for fame or pleasure.

(84)
A truly wise one
doesn’t do wrong to win anything—
not for power,
not for money,
not even for their own family.
They rise above that.

(85)
Most people stay stuck
on the near shore of life—
just running back and forth.
Only a few ever cross to the far side.

(86)
But those who walk the real path—
the deep path—
they cross the river of death.
They reach the other side.

(87–88)
So leave the dark road behind.
Take the bright path.
Go from householder to wanderer.
Detach.
Crave nothing.
Clean the mind like a temple.
That joy of freedom?
Few ever taste it,
but it’s worth everything.

(89)
Those whose minds are fully awakened,
who’ve let go of craving,
and shine with wisdom—
they don’t wait for heaven.
They live in freedom right now.



This copy of the Dhammapada is paraphrased and modernized from the translation by Acharya Buddharakkhita. (Originally published by Buddha Dharma Education Association Inc).
