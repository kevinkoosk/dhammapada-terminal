# Chapter 23: Like an Elephant in the Storm

![](images/image-5.jpg)

(320)
Like an elephant who stands his ground
while arrows fly from every side,
so too will I endure harsh words—
because the world is full of people
who’ve lost their way.

(321)
A trained elephant brings pride to a king.
But the best among humans
is the one who stays calm
even when insulted.

(322)
Yes, well-bred horses, mighty tuskers,
and noble mules are impressive—
but even more admirable
is the person who’s tamed their own mind.

(323)
You can’t ride beasts to the land beyond sorrow.
Only self-mastery
can carry you to true peace.

(324)
There’s a tusker named Dhanapalaka—
once wild and majestic, now chained.
He no longer eats.
He just stares into space,
longing for the forest.

(325)
But humans, too, can sink into that stupor—
lazy, greedy, rolling in comfort like fat pigs.
And so, they are reborn again, and again.

(326)
Once, my mind wandered wherever it pleased.
Now, with wisdom,
I guide it firmly—
like a trainer with a sharp hook
taming an elephant in heat.

(327)
Wake up!
Watch your thoughts!
Pull yourself out of the mud—
just like an elephant
rising from a swamp.

(328)
If you find a wise and kind friend—
someone walking the right path—
stay close.
Even if the way is rough,
walk it together with joy.

(329)
But if you can’t find such a friend,
don’t settle for less.
Go your own way,
like a lone elephant
leaving behind a conquered land.

(330)
It’s better to walk alone
than keep company with fools.
Be free, do no harm,
live like a wild elephant
at peace in the forest.

(331)
A true friend shows up when it matters.
Being content is a blessing.
When life ends, good deeds matter most.
Letting go of suffering—
that’s the greatest gain.

(332)
Be good to your parents.
Respect the monks.
Serve the wise.
This is the good life.

(333)
Hold fast to virtue,
have unshakable faith,
seek wisdom,
and walk away from wrongdoing.
These are the roots of a beautiful life.
