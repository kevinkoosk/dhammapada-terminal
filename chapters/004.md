# Chapter 4: Don’t Just Pick Flowers

![](images/image-4.jpg)

(44)
Who can rise above this world—
past gods and men and death itself?
Who can master the path of wisdom
like a florist shaping flowers into something beautiful?

(45)
The one who strives.
The one who walks the path with care.
They shape their life like a garland—
deliberate, graceful, complete.

(46)
This body is just foam.
A trick of the light.
So see through the illusion,
pull out the arrows of craving,
and walk away from Death’s reach.

(47)
Like a flash flood
sweeping through a sleeping village—
death comes fast
for those lost in pleasure.

(48)
The one who can’t stop chasing desires
gets caught every time.
Mara knows that game too well.

(49)
But the wise?
They take only what they need—
like a bee collecting nectar
without harming the flower.

(50)
Don’t waste time judging others.
Don’t obsess over their flaws.
Look at yourself.
Fix what’s yours to fix.

(51)
Words can be pretty—
but without action, they’re empty.
Like a flower with no scent.

(52)
But when your actions match your words—
that’s real beauty.
That’s a flower with fragrance.

(53)
From a mountain of flowers,
so many garlands can be made.
From this short life,
so many good things can bloom.

(54)
The smell of flowers and spices
can only drift so far.
But the scent of virtue—
it spreads in all directions.

(55)
Sandalwood, jasmine, lotus—
their scent is lovely.
But the fragrance of a good heart?
It’s beyond compare.

(56)
Earthly scents fade.
But goodness?
It rises even to the gods.

(57)
Mara can’t follow the truly virtuous—
those who stay mindful and walk in truth.

(58)
Even in a pile of trash
a lotus can bloom—pure and bright.

(59)
Just like that,
amid the mess of the world,
a true follower of the Buddha
shines with quiet wisdom.



This copy of the Dhammapada is paraphrased and modernized from the translation by Acharya Buddharakkhita. (Originally published by Buddha Dharma Education Association Inc).
