# Chapter 19: The Just

![](images/image-1.jpg)

(256)
You’re not just
just because you say so.
A wise person digs deep—
into what’s right
and what’s not.

(257)
Someone who judges with truth,
not with ego or bias—
that’s the real guardian of justice.

(258)
You’re not wise just because you talk a lot.
Wisdom is quiet, calm, and brave.
That’s who we call wise.

(259)
Quoting scriptures doesn’t mean
you understand them.
But someone who hears even a little
and lives by it—
they get it.

(260)
Gray hair doesn’t make an elder.
Some people just grow old,
not wise.

(261)
But someone honest, gentle,
disciplined, and clear—
who’s freed their mind—
that’s a true elder.

(262)
Charm and sweet talk mean nothing
if your heart is twisted by jealousy or deceit.

(263)
But if you've burned away
greed, hate, and lies—
you're the real deal.

(264)
Shaving your head doesn’t make you a monk.
If you're full of cravings and lies,
you’re just playing dress-up.

(265)
But if you’ve beaten down
both small and big evils—
then you're a true monk.

(266)
Begging for alms doesn’t make you holy.
Wearing robes doesn’t make you wise.

(267)
If you’ve let go of gain and loss,
if you walk the path with understanding—
you’re a monk, no matter what they call you.

(268)
Silence alone doesn’t make a sage.
You could be quiet and still be lost.
But if you weigh every thought with care—
that’s wisdom.

(269)
A true sage
lets go of what’s wrong
and sees the big picture.
They walk with clear eyes
in both this world and the next.

(270)
Hurting others doesn’t make you noble.
Being harmless does.

(271–272)
Don’t settle.
Don’t think,
“I’ve followed the rules, learned enough,
found bliss others don’t know.”
Until your heart is truly free,
keep going.



This copy of the Dhammapada is paraphrased and modernized from the translation by Acharya Buddharakkhita. (Originally published by Buddha Dharma Education Association Inc).
